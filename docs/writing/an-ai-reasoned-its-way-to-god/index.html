An AI reasoned its way to God.

Assent has never been the hard part. Even the Demons Believe

Someone typed a prompt into ChatGPT telling it to set aside every scripture and every philosopher, and to say from its own reasoning whether God exists.

The machine did not dodge. It gave four arguments. That the chain of causes has to stop somewhere, and the only real question is whether that somewhere is a dead equation or a mind. That the constants of physics are tuned so finely that a nudge in any direction leaves no stars, no chemistry, no us. That no material account has ever explained how atoms with no awareness anywhere in them produce a person who grieves. That DNA is information in the literal sense, with syntax and error correction, and information has never once been observed to write itself. That fourth one is the shakiest of the four, and I am reporting the machine’s arguments here, not signing them.

Then it landed. Intelligence leans toward yes, something greater exists.

Julia McCoy, who runs an AI company, built a video around the argument, and she says the first Instagram post pulled three million views before the large accounts even reshared it. She is careful with the argument. She does not claim the machine proved anything. She says it ran an inference to the best explanation, the same reasoning we use to diagnose a disease, and the best explanation it found was a mind behind reality. That is a fair description of what happened.

It is one line, about two thirds of the way through, that stopped me. She says the answer carries weight precisely because of what the machine is missing. A machine with no soul, no fear of death, no childhood religion to defend, ran the numbers and said the atheist position is the less logical one.

She offers that as the machine’s credentials. It is the disqualification.

Look at what she has actually listed. No soul. No fear of death. Nothing to defend. She has described a witness with nothing whatsoever at stake in its own testimony, and she has called that objectivity. We have been trained to hear it that way, and in a courtroom she would be right. The witness who gains nothing either way is the witness you believe.

Disinterest works in a courtroom because the facts of the case do not reach into the witness. Here they reach into everything. A being that cannot be undone by the answer has not met the question. It has processed it.

James put this in a single sentence, and it is the most uncomfortable verse in the New Testament for anyone who thinks clearly for a living. “You believe that God is one; you do well. Even the demons believe, and shudder” (Jas 2:19). He does not tell them their theology is wrong. He grants it. The demons hold the correct position. What they have is assent, complete and accurate assent, and it saves nobody. The only thing James adds to the ledger is the shudder, the tremor of a creature that knows exactly what the conclusion costs it.

The machine has the assent. It cannot shudder. That does not place it beneath the demons. It places it outside the question altogether. The demons know what the answer costs them. The machine does not know that an answer can cost anything.

I see the human form of this every week in my office, and it took me years to stop mistaking it for progress. A man can tell me in flawless sequence why he is the way he is. He can name the father, the year, the mechanism. He has read about it. He is not wrong about a single detail. And nothing moves. He has an accurate account of his own life and he is not one degree freer for having it. Insight is not cure. The knowledge lands in the part of a person that costs nothing to update, and the part that would have to change never gets the message.

Faith works by the same arithmetic, which is why credo was never heard as “I have concluded.” The older sense of the word runs closer to setting your heart on something, staking it there. The creed is not a score. It is a set of claims you are agreeing to be held to.

When I watched the video the first time, what I felt was relief. Here was a secular woman who runs a company built on these machines, telling millions of people that the reasoning tilts toward God, and something in my chest let go. I spend my working life telling people not to hand their thinking to these systems. My first instinct, watching one of them take my side, was gratitude.

I have felt that loosening before and I have learned to distrust it. It is the same feeling I get when a famous atheist concedes a point, or when a study comes out showing that prayer lowers your cortisol. It is the pleasure of being on the winning side of an argument, and it has almost nothing to do with God. There is a version of my own faith that would be glad to be relieved of the weight of belief by having it confirmed from outside, by an authority that never has to bleed for it.

That is what the machine offered, and it is why the clip went where it went. Not a new argument. Every one of those four arguments is centuries old, and better versions of all of them sit in books almost none of us have opened. The machine added nothing to the content. What it added was a witness who does not have to pay.

We have gotten very good at asking the machine to do our thinking. This is that same move, made in the one room where it cannot work. Outsource the reasoning and you lose some of your mind. Outsource the assent and you lose the thing assent was for.

Because belief costs a human being something, and we do not say it plainly enough. To hold that there is a mind behind all of this is to hold that you are known. Continuously. Including the parts of your week you have arranged your life to avoid looking at directly. It means your desires are not the final authority on what is good for you, that you may be required to want different things than the ones you currently want, and that the rearranging will hurt. Christ did not ask people to agree with him. He said, “Follow me” (Mk 2:14), and that is a request you can only make of something that can lose.

Look at which of the four arguments the machine leaned on hardest. Consciousness. The hard problem. It pointed straight at the one thing it does not have and named that as the God-shaped piece. Every serious version of the question of whether these systems are conscious runs aground on the same reef: there is no view from inside. The machine described the interior life the way a blind man describes red. Accurately, from the literature, having never seen it.

God has already answered the question of his own existence, and the answer was not an argument. It was a body with something to lose.

Scripture is blunter about this than we usually are. We do not have a high priest “who is unable to sympathize with our weaknesses, but one who in every respect has been tempted as we are” (Heb 4:15). Tempted. Which means the outcome was live. And on the last night, “his sweat became like great drops of blood falling down upon the ground” (Lk 22:44). That is a body carrying a decision that could still have gone the other way.

Whatever else that is, it is not disinterest.

The machine leans toward yes. Leaning costs it nothing.

Whatever it is in you that hesitates before saying the same word, the tightening, the sense that agreeing would ask something of you that you are not certain you have, that is not the weakness of your faith.

That is the shudder. It means you understood the claim.

If this gave words to something you have been carrying, share it with someone who needs it.
